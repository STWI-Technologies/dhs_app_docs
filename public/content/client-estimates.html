Overview

An estimate is a price before the work. It says what the provider will do, what it costs, and how long that price holds. Nothing is committed until you answer it.

This is the part of the portal where a decision is actually yours, so it is worth knowing what the buttons do.

Reading an estimate

Opening one shows the whole quote, not a summary:

  • Overview: the property, the crew, when it was created, and when the price expires. Some estimates say Never expires instead of a date.
  • Services and Products: every line, with its rate, its time or quantity, and its own total. This is where a price stops being one number.
  • Checklist: when the work carries one, the steps that will be followed.
  • Recurring Schedule: for work meant to repeat, how often.
  • Billing information: where the bill would go. There is an alternate billing section for when that is not you.
  • Deposit: whether one is required before the work starts, or none is.
  • Chat: questions about this estimate, kept with it.

Under the lines sit subtotal, discount, tax and total, so you can see what the tax is being charged on rather than only the figure at the bottom.

Approving it

Approve Estimate is your yes. The provider can then turn it into a job with everything carried across, which is why approving matters more than replying "looks good" in a message.

Your signature

Approval asks you to upload your signature as an image file. You pick the file, the portal shows you a preview before it goes, and the supported formats and the size limit are stated on the panel. If the file is too large or is not an image, it says so rather than failing quietly.

That signature is what makes the approval a record both sides can point at later.

Saying no, or not yet

Reject Estimate asks for a reason. Filling it in honestly is worth the thirty seconds: "too expensive" and "wrong dates" lead to very different next quotes, and the reason goes to the provider rather than into a void.

If what you want is a different price rather than no work, say so in the reason or in the chat. The provider can edit and send it again, and it comes back to you as Ready for Review.

Adding a service yourself

An estimate is not read only. You can add a service to it from the provider's list, and edit one that is already there, which is the quickest way to say "while you are here, do this too" without starting a second request.

What you add changes the totals, and the provider sees it.

When an estimate expires

Prices do not hold forever. Once the expiration date passes, the estimate is Expired and the page tells you so, with a prompt to ask the provider to renew it. They can update anything that has moved and send it back for review.

Where estimates connect

  • Jobs: an approved estimate becomes one, with the same services, products and checklist.
  • Invoices: the bill comes from the job, so the price you approved is the price you are billed.
  • Inbox: every estimate has its own conversation.
