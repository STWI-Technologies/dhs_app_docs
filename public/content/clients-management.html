Clients Management

Overview

A client is a customer you do business with — a homeowner or a company. Every appointment, estimate, job, and invoice belongs to a client, and each client can have as many properties as they own.

From this section you can:

  • Add clients with their contact details, first property, and billing address.
  • Invite clients to connect to your account through the client portal, and see who has accepted.
  • Edit a client's contact and billing information.
  • Archive clients you no longer work with, and restore them later.
  • Manage their properties — add, edit, or remove them, and open one on the map.
  • See everything related to a client — appointments, estimates, jobs, and invoices, all in one place.
  • Message them without leaving the client record.
  • Keep files against the client and against each property.
  • Import and export your client list as CSV.

Important: clients are never deleted, only archived. Archiving hides a client from your active list while keeping every appointment, estimate, job, and invoice they were part of. You can restore an archived client at any time.

The Clients list

To open it, go to Menu > Clients.

Connected, Invites, Archived

Three tabs divide your clients by their relationship with you, not by their type:

TabWho's in it
ConnectedThe clients you work with — the ones you added yourself, plus the ones who accepted your invitation. This is where you start.
InvitesClients you've invited to the portal who haven't accepted yet, and the ones who declined.
ArchivedClients you've archived.

To filter by Residential or Business, use the Filters panel described below — client type is a filter now, not a tab.

Search and Filters

The card header holds a search box — it matches names, company names and email addresses, and narrows the list as you type — and a Filters button.

FilterWhat it narrows to
CityClients with a property in the cities you pick.
StateClients with a property in the states you pick.
Property TypeResidential or Business clients.
TagsClients carrying the tags you pick.

Filters combine, and they combine with search and with the tab you're on. The number on the Filters button tells you how many are active, so you always know why the list looks short — and Reset Filter at the top of the panel clears them in one go.

The three-dot menu

  • Start a Conversation — opens your thread with this client, creating one if you've never spoken.
  • Attachments — the client's files.
  • Archive, or Restore if the client is already archived.

On a client who hasn't accepted your invitation yet, these are all greyed out — there's nothing to manage until they accept.

Sorting and pages

Click the Client Name or Type header to sort by it, and again to reverse. The list shows 10 clients per page; switch to 20, 50, or 100 at the bottom left. Changing a tab, a filter, or the search takes you back to the first page.

The guided tour

The first time you open the Clients list, a short tour points out the tabs, the Add Client button, search, Filters, and the row actions. Skip dismisses it and it won't come back.

Adding a client

Click Add Client. A panel slides in from the right with two steps.

Step 1 — Client Contact Information

FieldRequiredNotes
First Name / Last NameYesThe person you deal with.
Phone numberYesWith a country selector, set to the US by default.
Email AddressYesHas to be a real address, and it's how the client signs in to the portal if you invite them.
Company NameNoFill it in for a business client; it shows under their name in the list.
Company email / Company phoneNoThe business's own contact details, when they differ from the person's — a main office line, an accounts inbox.
WebsiteNo
TagsNoType a tag and press Enter. Tags are how you filter the list later — trade, territory, priority, whatever suits you.

You can also attach files to the client here, before it even exists.

If the email already belongs to a client, the panel tells you instead of creating a duplicate. What happens next depends on the situation: if they're already connected to you, you're pointed at the existing record; if they exist in DHS but aren't connected to you, the step is replaced by their details and a Send invite button; and if you've already invited them, you get the option to send the invitation again.

Step 2 — Property info & Review

The client's first property comes next: its type (Residential or Business), address, city, state, zip code, and optionally a timezone and a photo of the property. A picture helps crews recognise the place when they arrive — up to 4 MB, in JPEG, PNG or WebP.

Below that is the billing address. It's set to match the property address, so leave it alone unless invoices go somewhere else — a management company, a head office — in which case turn it off and type the billing address in.

At the bottom, a summary of everything you've entered. Check it and click Add Client.

You can move between the two steps with Next Step and the back arrow, and Cancel closes the panel without saving.

Inviting a client to the portal

Clients can have their own portal account, where they see their estimates, jobs and invoices and message you directly. That connection starts with an invitation from you.

An invited client sits on the Invites tab, with a badge on their name:

  • Invitation Pending — they haven't answered yet.
  • Invite declined — they said no. The record stays on the Invites tab for reference.

While an invitation is unanswered you can't act on that client: opening the row shows a read-only summary of their contact details instead of the full record, and the menu actions are greyed out. Once they accept, they move to Connected and everything unlocks.

Once a client uses their portal, they own their own details. Their name, phone, email and properties become read-only for you, and the app says so: "This client updates their own details from their portal." You can still archive them, message them, and keep working — you just can't edit information they now maintain themselves. Ask them to change it on their side.

The client record

Click a client to open their record.

The header carries the client's name, whether they're Active or Archived, and a three-dot menu with Attachments and Archive or Restore. A Start a Conversation button appears there too, until you've started one — after that, the conversation itself lives in the sidebar.

Client Contact Information, on the right, holds their name and type, phone, email and billing address. The pencil in its corner opens the edit panel.

Underneath it, three tabs:

  • Chat — the conversation, right there. Client is your thread with them; Team is the internal one about this client, which the client never sees.
  • Notes — anything your crews should know before they arrive: gate codes, a dog, who to ask for on site. Internal too.
  • Attachments — the client's files.

Four tabs hold their work, each with a count:

TabWhat's in it
PropertiesEvery property the client owns. See below.
AppointmentsTheir appointments, past and upcoming. Click one to open it.
EstimatesTheir estimates, and a button to write a new one with the client already filled in.
Job & InvoicesTheir jobs and invoices, with buttons to start either one for this client.

Starting an estimate, job, or invoice from here saves you re-picking the client — and when they only have one property, that gets picked too.

Properties

A client can own any number of properties, all managed from the Properties tab of their record.

Each card shows the property's type, its photo, and its full address, plus:

  • View on Map — opens the Map on that address.
  • Attachments — files for this property alone: photos, plans, permits. Kept separately from the client's own files.
  • Edit — the same fields you filled in when you created the client's first property.
  • Delete — removes the property for good, after asking you to confirm.

Two things stop a delete. A client must keep at least one property, so the last one can't be removed — add the replacement first, then delete the old one. And if the client manages their own data through the portal, their properties are theirs to change.

Before deleting, check whether any appointment, estimate, or job still points at that address.

Use Add Property to add another. If a client has none yet, the tab says so and explains why you'd want one: without a property there's nowhere to schedule work.

Editing a client

Open the client and click Edit in the sidebar, or use the button in the Actions column of the list. The panel has two steps this time:

  • Client Contact Information — name, phone, email, company, website, tags.
  • Billing — the billing address, with a summary underneath.

Properties aren't here: you edit those on the Properties tab, where each one has its own form. Same for attachments — those live on the client record. Click Apply Changes to save.

If Edit is greyed out, the client has portal access and maintains their own details.

Archiving and restoring

Archiving takes a client off your active list without losing anything they were part of.

  1. Open the three-dot menu on their row, or the one in the header of their record.
  2. Choose Archive.
  3. Confirm.

They move to the Archived tab, and their appointments, estimates, jobs and invoices stay exactly as they were. To bring them back, find them on the Archived tab, open the three-dot menu, choose Restore, and confirm.

Attachments

Attachments can hang off a client or off one of their properties. Contracts, signed agreements and paperwork belong to the client; photos, plans and permits usually belong to a property.

Open Attachments from the three-dot menu on their row, or from the menu in the header of their record. Drag files onto the panel or click to browse; each one shows its progress, and a failed upload offers a retry. Property attachments work the same way, from the paperclip on the property card.

Deleting a file is permanent.

Importing clients from a CSV

Moving in from another system, or starting with a spreadsheet? Import CSV takes the whole list at once.

  1. Upload your file. Drag the CSV in or browse for it. No file ready? Download the sample and fill it in — it already has the right headers.
  2. Match your columns to DHS fields. Your spreadsheet's headings are listed next to the fields they could fill. Company Name and Email are the two that must be mapped; everything else — website, up to two properties with their addresses and contacts, the billing address, tags, timezone — is up to you.
  3. Check the preview. The rows are shown as they'll be imported, with anything invalid called out: a missing required value, a malformed email, a property type that isn't Residential or Commercial, an unknown state code. Fix your file and upload it again if you need to.
  4. Import. Large files carry on in the background, so you can keep working. You'll be told when it's done, and if some rows failed, how many and why.

Exporting your client list

Click Export CSV and confirm. You get a spreadsheet of exactly what the list is showing — so set the tab, search and filters first, and the export follows. On the Business filter, that's your commercial clients only.

The button is greyed out when there's nothing to export.

Note: Import CSV and Export CSV can be turned off for your account, in which case neither button appears.

Clients added in a hurry

When you're writing an estimate or booking a job for someone who isn't in DHS yet, you don't have to stop and fill in a full client record — Quick Add takes a name and enough to keep going.

Those clients show a marker next to their name in the list, offering Complete Info. It opens their record so you can fill in the rest — the property address, billing, the details that invoices and scheduling need. The marker disappears once you save.
