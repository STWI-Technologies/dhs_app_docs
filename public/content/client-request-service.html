Overview

Request Service is how you ask a provider to do something. It is a single form, and what comes out of it is a request the provider can turn into an appointment, an estimate or a job.

Get to it from the menu, or from the button beside your name on the dashboard.

Filling it in

Who you are asking

Pick the service provider first. Everything after it depends on that choice, because the services you can pick from are theirs. The form says so if you try to skip ahead.

If you are connected to only one provider, this is one click and you move on.

Where and what

FieldRequiredWhat goes in it
PropertyYesWhich of your properties the work is at. Add properties in Settings.
ServiceYesFrom that provider's list. If the list is empty, they have not published their services yet.
NameYesA short title, so you can recognise it later in your lists.
DescriptionNoOptional, and worth writing anyway. See below.
FrequencyNoFor work that repeats rather than happening once.

The description is where a request stops being a guess for the provider. "Kitchen tap dripping, worse in the morning, under the sink is dry" gets a better answer than "plumbing". They quote from what you write.

When

Give a preferred date and time, and a service window around it. The window matters more than the exact time: it is what lets them fit you into a route instead of a slot.

There is also an alternate date and time with its own window. Filling it in is the single thing most likely to get you booked sooner, because it gives them two ways to say yes.

Sending it

Submit sends it. If anything required is missing the form tells you which field before it goes.

After that it is with the provider. What you see next depends on what they do with it: an appointment if they want to come and look, an estimate if they can quote from what you wrote, or a job if it is clear enough to simply book.

Where requests connect

  • Appointments: a visit to work out what is needed.
  • Estimates: a price for you to approve.
  • Jobs: the work itself, once it is agreed.
  • Inbox: where to chase or clarify, rather than sending a second request.
