Invoices Management

Overview

An invoice is the bill. It comes from the work, usually straight from a completed job, goes out to the client, and then you track it until the money arrives.

From this section you can:

  • Raise invoices, from a job or on their own.
  • Send them to the client by email or text.
  • See what's been read: sent, viewed, paid.
  • Record payments, including part payments, with the method and reference.
  • Chase what's late, by due date and balance.
  • Download a PDF for clients who need one.

The Invoices list

To open it, go to Menu > Invoices.

Sorting by Due date and by Total Balance is the whole of credit control: the first tells you what's late, the second tells you what's worth chasing first.

Creating an invoice

Most invoices shouldn't be typed at all. Open the completed job, go to its Invoices tab, and use Add Invoice from Job. The services, products and totals come straight across.

To raise one directly, click Add Invoice on the Invoices list and fill in the client, the dates, and the lines.

A job can carry more than one invoice, which is how you bill in stages: a deposit up front, the balance on completion.

The invoice

Click an invoice to open it.

The header has its number and status, a Send Invoice button, and a three-dot menu holding Edit invoice, Download Pdf and Delete. Two tabs sit below. Job Details holds who it's billed to, the job it came from, and the services and products with their totals. Payments is where money received is recorded.

The client sidebar keeps their contact details to hand for when you need to chase.

Sending an invoice

Send Invoice emails or texts it to the client, with a preview of what they'll receive first. The status moves to Sent, and to Viewed once they open it.

Recording a payment

Open the Payments tab and use Add Payment to record money received:

FieldNotes
AmountWhat was received. Less than the total is fine. That's a part payment, and the balance keeps tracking the rest.
Payment MethodCash, Check, Credit Card, Bank Transfer, or Other.
Transaction dateWhen it was received, which isn't always today.
Payment referenceA cheque number, a transfer reference, whatever you'd need to match it to a bank statement later.
StatusPending, Completed, Failed, Refunded, or Voided.
NotesAnything worth remembering about the payment.

Every payment is listed on the Payments tab and can be edited afterwards. The invoice's Balance Due updates as they come in, and once the balance reaches zero the invoice is Paid.

That status on the list is why part payments are worth recording as they arrive rather than at the end: an invoice showing a small remaining balance is a very different conversation from one showing the full amount.

Editing and deleting

A draft invoice is fully editable. Once it's been sent, and especially once a payment is against it, an invoice is a financial record, so what you can change narrows. Correcting a sent invoice usually means issuing a new one rather than quietly rewriting the old.

Delete is available from the row in the list and from inside the invoice, but think twice about deleting anything a client has already seen or paid.

Sharing a PDF

Download Pdf gives you the invoice as a document, for clients who file paper or need to pass it to an accounts department.

Where invoices come from

  • Jobs: the usual route. The Invoices tab on a job raises one from the work, and a job can have several.
  • Estimates: an estimate becomes a job, and the job becomes the invoice. The Linked to column keeps the trail.
  • Clients: a client's record lists their invoices, so you can see everything they owe in one place.
  • Services and products: the lines, with tax applied to whatever is marked taxable.
