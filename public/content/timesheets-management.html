Timesheets

Overview

A timesheet collects one person's time for a period, entry by entry, and carries it through submission and approval. Most of the entries are not typed: they arrive on their own when someone adds a visit to a job or an appointment, or starts a timer.

From this section you can:

  • See where the hours went, split into en route, job, job prep and office time.
  • Add time by hand for anything the timers missed.
  • Link loose entries to the right timesheet.
  • Submit, approve and reject, with the rules about who can do which.
  • Compare with last week, from the cards at the top.

The four kinds of time

Every entry is one of four types, and the type is what makes the totals mean something.

TypeWhat it coversNeeds a job
En RouteTravelling to the work.Yes
Work TimeThe work itself, on site.Yes
Job PrepGetting ready for a job: loading, picking up parts, paperwork before the visit.Yes
Office TimeEverything not tied to a job.No

The Timesheets list

To open it, go to Menu > Timesheets.

Five cards sit above the list: Total Hours, Total en route hours, Total office time, Total job time and Total job prep hours. Each one carries its movement against the week before, so a number that jumps is visible without opening anything.

ColumnWhat it shows
UserWhose time it is. On the Solo plan this column is not there, because there is only one of you.

Office TimeHours with no job attached.
Job TimeHours on the work itself.

Search sits in the card header. Beside it are two filters: Users, and Status. Both take more than one value at a time and both have a reset.

An approved timesheet cannot be deleted. Its bin is greyed and says so when you hover it. That is deliberate: an approved timesheet is a payroll record.

Creating a timesheet

Click Add Timesheet. It asks for three things:

  • Date, required.
  • User, required, and fixed from then on. You cannot move a timesheet to another person after it is created, so check it before saving. Crew members can only create their own.
  • Description, optional, and worth filling in because it becomes the title you see at the top of the timesheet.

In practice you will create fewer of these than you think. With automatic linking on, the first entry of the week makes its own draft.

Inside a timesheet

Open one and the header carries the person's name and the status. If you are an admin or the owner, the status is a dropdown you can change directly; everyone else sees it as a label. Add Time sits on the right, and disappears once the timesheet is approved.

Overview holds the description, the user, their crew and the date it was created, with Edit for the date and description.

Time Entries is the timesheet itself: the entries linked to it, with a search box and an activity filter that narrows the table to one type of time. The table shows the job, the user, the date, the description, the type as a coloured tag and the duration, and totals at the bottom. Each row can be edited or deleted from its Actions.

Adding time by hand

Add Time asks for the user, the date, the job, a From and To, and a description. Four rules are worth knowing before you start typing:

  • The job is required for En Route, Work Time and Job Prep. For Office Time there is no job to give.
  • The description is required. An entry without one tells nobody anything two weeks later.
  • To has to be after From, and the duration cannot be zero.
  • Neither time can be in the future.

Unlinked entries

Below the entries sits Unlinked Entries: time that exists but belongs to no timesheet yet. It appears there when someone adds a visit to a job or an appointment, or starts a timer, and nothing picked it up.

Click one and the app asks: link to this timesheet, or select another? Link puts it here. Select another opens Link entry, where you either search for an existing timesheet or make a new one by giving a date and a user.

Linking runs backwards too: Unlink on a linked entry sends it back to the unlinked pile, after a confirmation.

Crew members only see their own unlinked entries. Admins and owners see everyone's, which is what makes this the place to tidy up the week.

Automatic linking

Most accounts never touch the unlinked list, because linking is automatic. The switch lives in Settings > Timesheet and works like this:

  • When an entry is created or a timer starts, the app checks whether automatic linking applies to that person.
  • If it does, it looks for a draft timesheet for the same week, Monday to Sunday.
  • If there is one, the entry joins it. If there is not, a draft is created and the entry goes in.

The main switch is account wide. Underneath it, each person can opt themselves out with Disable automatic linking for myself, which leaves the rest of the team on the account default.

Submitting and approving

A timesheet moves through four states: Draft, Pending, then Approved or Rejected.

ActionWho can do itWhen
Submit TimesheetThe person the timesheet belongs toWhile it is a Draft
Change to DraftThe person the timesheet belongs toWhile it is Pending, to pull it back and fix something
Approve TimesheetAn admin or owner who is not the person it belongs toWhile it is Pending
Reject TimesheetAn admin or owner who is not the person it belongs toWhile it is Pending

Nobody approves their own timesheet. An admin looking at their own sees Submit, not Approve.

These buttons only appear once the timesheet has time linked to it. An empty timesheet has nothing to submit, so the strip under the table stays out of the way.
