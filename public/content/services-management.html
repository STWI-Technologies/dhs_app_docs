Services Management

Overview

A service is labour you charge for — an inspection, an install, a repair, a callout. You define it once with its hourly rate, and from then on you pick it when building an appointment, an estimate, a job, or an invoice, instead of retyping the price every time.

From this section you can:

  • Add services with a rate, a description, and any documents that go with them.
  • Edit one when your pricing changes.
  • Archive services you no longer offer, and restore them later.
  • Delete an archived service for good, when nothing references it.
  • Decide what clients see — which services appear in your client portal.
  • Search and filter by tag, group, whether it's taxable, and whether clients can see it.
  • Import and export your service list as CSV.
  • Keep attachments — spec sheets, manuals, safety documents.

Important: archiving and deleting are different. Archiving takes a service out of the pickers while keeping every record that used it — that's what you want when you stop offering something. Deleting is permanent, and only possible once nothing references the service.

The Services list

To open it, go to Menu > Inventory > Services.

Active and Archived

Two tabs: Active, the services you currently offer, and Archived, the ones you've retired. Archived services don't appear when you're building an estimate or a job, but they stay on everything they were already part of.

Columns

Row actions

Each row has an eye that opens View Service Details — everything about the service, read-only — and a three-dot menu with the rest:

  • Edit service — the same form you filled in when you created it.
  • Attachments — its documents.
  • Archive on the Active tab; Restore Service and Delete on the Archived tab.

Delete is greyed out while anything references the service: "This item can't be deleted because it's linked to existing records." Archive it instead — that's what archiving is for.

Search and filters

The search box in the card header matches names and SKUs. Filter opens a panel with four ways to narrow the list:

FilterWhat it narrows to
TagsServices carrying the tags you pick.
GroupServices in the groups you pick.
TaxableOnly tax-applicable items.
Client PortalOnly the ones visible in the client portal.

Reset Filter clears them all. Filters, search, and the tab you're on all work together.

The guided tour

The first time you open the list, a short tour points out the parts of the page. Skip dismisses it for good.

Adding a service

Click Add Service. A panel opens from the right, titled Add New Service, with everything on one screen.

FieldRequiredNotes
SKUYesA code unique to this service. Pick something you'll recognise — INSP-001, HVAC-TUNE. You can't change it afterwards, so it's worth a moment's thought. If the code is already taken you'll be told when you leave the field.
NameYesWhat it's called on estimates and invoices, so write it the way a client should read it: "Annual HVAC Inspection", not "insp".
Cost per hourYesYour hourly rate. Two decimal places.
DescriptionNoWhat the service includes, how long it usually takes, anything that saves a phone call later.
TagsNoType a tag and press Enter. Tags are what you filter by later, so a little consistency here pays off.

Two switches sit below the fields:

  • Taxable — "Include this service in tax calculations when applicable." Turn it on and tax is worked out automatically wherever the service is used.
  • Show on client portal — clients can see this service and request it themselves. Leave it off for anything you'd rather quote by hand.

You can attach documents while creating the service — spec sheets, manuals, anything a crew might need on site. Then click Add Service.

Editing a service

Open the three-dot menu on the row and choose Edit service. Everything is editable except the SKU. Save with Apply Changes.

The edit panel also carries the Archive button (or Delete, for a service already archived), which asks you to confirm right there — handy when you've opened a service and realised it's one you no longer offer.

Attachments aren't in the edit form: once a service exists, its documents are managed from Attachments in the three-dot menu.

Attachments

Each service can carry its own files. Open Attachments from the three-dot menu, then drag files in or click to browse. From the same panel you can search the list, download a file, or delete one. Deleting is permanent.

Archiving, restoring, deleting

To archive: three-dot menu > Archive > confirm. The service moves to the Archived tab and stops appearing in pickers. Everything that already used it is untouched.

To restore: Archived tab > three-dot menu > Restore Service. It's back in the pickers immediately.

To delete for good: Archived tab > three-dot menu > Delete > confirm. Only available when the In Use column is empty. If it isn't, the service is on a record somewhere and archiving is the right answer.

Importing and exporting

Export CSV downloads your service list as a spreadsheet — useful for a price review, or for sharing your catalogue with someone who doesn't use DHS.

Import CSV takes a list in. Upload the file, match your columns to the DHS fields, check the preview, and import. Rows that fail validation are reported so you can fix the file and try again.

Note: both buttons can be turned off for your account, in which case neither appears.

Services added in a hurry

Mid-estimate and the service you need doesn't exist yet? Quick Add — from the top bar, or from inside the service picker — asks for a SKU, a name, and a price, creates it, and drops you back where you were. Fill in the rest later; the row carries a marker until you do.

Where services are used

  • Estimates — the line items you quote. The rate comes across automatically.
  • Jobs — the work being done, and what it's worth.
  • Invoices — what the client is billed for, with tax applied to the services marked taxable.
  • Appointments — the work planned for a visit.
  • Your client portal — the services you marked visible are the ones clients can request themselves.

Because everything points back at the same service, changing its rate changes what future records pick up — records already written keep the price they were created with.
