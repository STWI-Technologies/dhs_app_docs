User Management

Overview

This is where you manage everyone who has access to your DHS account — your office staff and your field crews. Each user gets a role that decides what they can reach, and can belong to one or more crews.

From this section you can:

  • Add users with their contact details, a role, and a password.
  • Edit anyone's profile, change their role, or update their photo.
  • Disable someone who should no longer get in, and enable them again later.
  • Send a password reset so they can set their own.
  • Search and filter by name, email, or role.

Important: users are never deleted, only disabled. Disabling stops someone signing in but keeps everything they did — their jobs, appointments and timesheets stay intact, and their name still shows on the records they worked. Enable them again and they're straight back in.

The Users list

To open it, go to Menu > Users.

Active and Disabled

Two tabs split the list: Active — everyone who can sign in right now — and Disabled, everyone whose access you've turned off. The card header names the tab you're on and counts it.

Columns

Row actions

On the Active tab:

ActionWhat it does
EditOpens their profile. Clicking their name does the same.
DisableAsks you to confirm, then takes away their access.

On the Disabled tab, Edit stays and Enable takes its place, to give someone their access back.

Two things stop a disable, and both are deliberate:

  • Your own row has no disable button at all. You can't lock yourself out.
  • Someone assigned to active jobs can't be disabled — the button is greyed out and reads "Cannot Disable User With Active Job Assignments". Reassign or finish their work first.

Search and the role filter

The search box in the card header matches names and email addresses. Next to it, Filter opens a panel where you tick the roles you want to see — pick several to combine them, and Reset Filter clears them.

Sorting and pages

Click the User, Email Address, or Role header to sort by it, and again to reverse. The Crew column isn't sortable. The list shows 10 users per page; switch to 20, 50, or 100 at the bottom left.

The guided tour

The first time you open the list, a short tour points out the Add User button, the tabs, the list, search, and the role filter. Skip dismisses it for good.

Roles

Every user has exactly one role. It's the single most important field on their profile, because it decides what they see when they sign in.

RoleTagWho it's for
OwnerGreyWhoever created the account. There's normally one, and you can't hand the role out — it isn't offered when you add or edit someone.
AdminGreenFull run of the platform: users, crews, settings, and the whole business side. The highest role you can give someone.
SupportYellowOffice and dispatch — scheduling, talking to clients, keeping the day moving, without full admin reach.
Crew MemberBlueThe field: technicians and installers who work jobs. This is what Quick Add gives a new user.

Only Owner, Admin and Crew Member users can be put on a crew. Support users don't appear in the crew member picker — they're office staff, not field staff.

Click Add User. A panel slides in from the right, titled Add New User.

The password

It needs at least 10 characters, and among them an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, a number, and a special character.

Rather than inventing one, use the generate button beside the field — it makes a password that already passes — then Copy if you want to pass it on yourself. Either way the password is emailed to the address you entered, so the new user gets it without you having to send anything.

Click Add User to finish.

Editing a user

Click the pencil on their row, or their name. The panel opens as Edit User with everything filled in: photo, name, email, phone, role, timezone, description.

The password fields aren't here — you don't get to see or set someone's password after the fact. Instead there's Reset Password, which emails them a link to choose a new one themselves. That section doesn't appear when you're editing your own profile.

To change someone's photo, Upload Image replaces it and the remove button clears it back to the default avatar.

Save with Apply Changes.

Turning access off and on

When someone leaves, or shouldn't be signing in for a while, disable them. Nothing they did is lost.

Disabling

From the Active tab, click the disable button on their row and confirm. You can also do it from inside their profile: the button sits at the top of the panel and asks you to confirm right there.

They move to the Disabled tab and can't sign in from that moment. Their history stays, and their name keeps showing on the work they did.

Remember the two cases that block it: your own account, and anyone with active job assignments.

Enabling

Switch to the Disabled tab, click enable on their row, and confirm — or open their profile and use the button at the top. They're back on the Active tab and can sign in immediately, with the same password as before.

Users added in a hurry

Building a crew and the person you need isn't a user yet? Quick Add — from the top bar, or from inside the crew member picker — takes just enough to create them and get you back to what you were doing.

They're created as a Crew Member, and their row carries a marker offering Complete Info until someone fills in the rest: their role if it should be different, their timezone, their phone. The marker clears when you save the full profile.

Where users show up

  • Crews — Owner, Admin and Crew Member users can be crew members, and one of them is the crew lead.
  • Appointments and jobs — work is assigned to crews, and their members are who turns up. Someone with active assignments can't be disabled.
  • Timesheets — hours are recorded per user, whether the timer ran or the entry was added by hand.
  • Messaging — users take part in internal team threads and in conversations with clients.
  • Reports — timesheet and performance reporting is broken down by user.
