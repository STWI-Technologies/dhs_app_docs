Appointments Management

Overview

An appointment is a shorter stop: a site visit, a walkthrough, a quote call, a check-in. It's the thing you book before you know what the work will be — and once you do know, it turns into an estimate or a job without being retyped.

From this section you can:

  • Book appointments with a client, a property, a crew and a time.
  • Send the details to the client and get them confirmed.
  • Follow them through — en route, arrived, completed, or a no-show.
  • Track time against the visit.
  • Turn one into an estimate, a job, or an invoice, and see everything that came out of it.

Booking an appointment

Click Add Appointment. You choose the client and which of their properties, what the visit is called, the crew, and the date and time.

The crew's availability is respected while you pick, so you can't quietly book someone who's already out on a job. If the client isn't in DHS yet, Quick Add is in the picker.

Appointments can also be created straight from the Scheduler by picking a slot, which is usually faster when you're booking around existing work.

The appointment

Click an appointment to open it.

The header has its name and status, a Start Timer action, and a three-dot menu. The sidebar keeps the client's phone and email to hand — this is the record you'll have open when you call to confirm.

Two tabs:

  • Overview — the appointment itself: the details, the property, and the services planned for the visit.
  • Related Records — everything that came out of it.

Sending it to the client

Send to Client sends the appointment details out by email or text, with a preview first. The status moves to Pending until they confirm.

This is what stops the "was anyone coming today?" call.

What comes out of an appointment

This is the part of the section that earns its keep. You went to look at the work; now you know what it is. The Related Records tab turns the visit into the next step, and then keeps the trail.

From an appointment you can createWhen
An estimateThe client wants a price before committing. Everything you gathered on the visit carries over.
A jobThe work is agreed and just needs scheduling.
An invoiceThe visit itself is what you're billing — a callout, an inspection, a diagnostic.

Once created, each one is listed on the same tab, so the appointment becomes the thread you can follow from "we went and looked" to "we were paid". Until then the sections say so plainly — nothing has come from this appointment yet.

Tracking time on a visit

Start Timer records time against the appointment, which matters for visits you bill by the hour and for knowing what unbilled site visits actually cost you. The hours land in Timesheets like any other time.

A note on products

Appointments carry services, not products — they're visits, not installs. If parts are involved, that's a job, and the appointment is where you create it from.

Editing, cancelling, deleting

Edit an appointment from its row or from inside it. If the time changes after the client has confirmed, send it again so they know.

Cancel when it isn't happening, and mark No Show when the client wasn't there — the distinction is worth keeping, because one is your change and the other is theirs. Delete is available too, but a cancelled appointment is a more honest record than a missing one.

Where appointments connect

  • Estimates, jobs and invoices — created from the appointment and listed on its Related Records tab.
  • Clients — every client's record lists their appointments.
  • Crews — assigned to the visit, and their availability is what the picker respects.
  • Scheduler and Map — the same appointments by time and by place.
  • Timesheets — time recorded against the visit.
  • Appointment Line — the phone line that books appointments for you. See its own article.
