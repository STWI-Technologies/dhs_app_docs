Jobs Management

Overview

A job is work you've committed to: a client, a property, a crew, a date, and the services and products involved. It's where an approved estimate ends up, where time gets recorded, and where the invoice comes from.

From this section you can:

  • Create jobs directly, or let them come from an approved estimate.
  • Schedule them to a crew, with a start and end date.
  • Follow them through — en route, arrived, started, paused, completed.
  • Record visits, so a job that takes several trips is tracked trip by trip.
  • Track time against the job, and against each visit.
  • Work a checklist, when the job has one.
  • Send the details to the client, and collect their feedback afterwards.
  • Invoice the work without re-entering a thing.
  • Set up recurring jobs for work that repeats.

Creating a job

Click Add Job. Three steps: the details — client, property, crew, services and products, and a checklist if there is one — then the start date, then the end date. Dates the crew isn't available are marked, so you don't double-book them.

Most jobs don't start here, though. If the work was quoted, open the approved estimate and use Convert to job — everything carries across.

The job

Click a job to open it.

The header carries its name, the status — which is also the control that moves the job along — and a three-dot menu with the rest of the actions. While the job is still open it also carries Start Timer; once the job is completed that button goes, since there is nothing left to time. The sidebar keeps the client's phone, email and billing address to hand.

Under the client's details the sidebar has three tabs: Chat, Notes and Attachments. Chat itself splits in two — Client and Team — so a message to the crew about access or parking doesn't go to the client by accident.

Visits

Not every job is one trip. A repair that needs a part ordered is two visits; a big install might be five. Visits is where each one is recorded, so the time and the story of the job stay straight instead of blurring into a single block of hours.

Add Visit records one: the date, the user who made it, the time from and to, and a description. Time is split by what it actually was:

  • Work Time — hands on the job.
  • Job Prep Time — getting ready, loading, picking up materials.
  • Office Time — paperwork and admin for this job.
  • En Route — travel.

Expand a visit to see its timesheet records, and to edit or delete it. One thing to know: a visit with a running timer can't be deleted — stop the timer first.

Tracking time

Start Timer on the job records time as it happens, which is more accurate than remembering at the end of the day. Crews normally run it from the mobile app while they work; the office can also add time by hand on a visit.

Either way the hours land in Timesheets, against the user and the job, which is what payroll and job profitability are built on.

Checklists

When a job carries a checklist, the Checklist tab holds the steps. Crews work through it on site and the job records what was done — useful for consistency, and for proving it afterwards.

Sending job details to the client

Send to Client — in the job's three-dot More Actions menu — sends the job's details out by email or text, with a preview of what they'll receive before it goes. That's what moves a job to Pending, waiting on their confirmation. It stays greyed out until the job has a date to tell the client about.

Completing a job

Mark the job Completed when the work is done — either from the status control beside its name, or with Complete job in the More Actions menu. If the job has a checklist, it needs to be worked through first; that's the point of having one.

Once complete, the client can be asked for feedback, which shows on the job's Overview and feeds the crew ratings you see on the Crews list.

Invoicing the work

Open the Invoices tab and use Add Invoice from Job. The services, products and totals come straight from the job — no retyping, and nothing forgotten in the transfer.

The button needs something to bill: "Add at least one service or product before creating an invoice." A job can carry more than one invoice, which is how staged or partial billing works.

Recurring jobs

Work that repeats — monthly maintenance, seasonal service — can run on a schedule instead of being created by hand each time. The Recurring Schedule tab is where the pattern lives, and it's also where you choose whether a change applies to this occurrence only or to the whole series. Worth being deliberate about, since one of those rewrites every future job.

Editing and deleting

What you can change depends on where the job is. A draft is fully editable; a job in progress or completed is progressively locked down, so history isn't quietly rewritten after the fact.

Edit sits at the top of the job's Overview card. It opens Edit job details — the client, title, property, crew, checklist and priority. Dates are not in there: moving a job in time is Reschedule, in the More Actions menu, and services and products are changed on the job itself.

Delete is at the bottom of the More Actions menu, and on the row in the list as a bin.

That menu also offers Cancel job, which is usually the more honest option. A job that's been invoiced or has time recorded against it is part of your records — cancelling keeps that history where deleting throws it away. On a job that's already completed, Delete is gone from the menu altogether and Cancel is what's left.

Where jobs connect

  • Estimates — an approved estimate becomes a job with everything carried over, and keeps a link back.
  • Appointments — a visit that turns into real work becomes a job.
  • Invoices — raised from the job, with its services and products.
  • Timesheets — every timer and manual entry lands there.
  • Checklists — attached to the job and worked on site.
  • Crews — assigned to the job; their availability is what the date pickers respect.
  • Scheduler and Map — the same jobs by time and by place.
