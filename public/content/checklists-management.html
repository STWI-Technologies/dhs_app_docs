Checklists

Overview

A checklist is a reusable list of steps. You build it once here, attach it to a job, and the crew works through it on site. It is how a job gets done the same way every time, whoever turns up.

From this section you can:

  • Build a checklist and its steps.
  • Put the steps in work order, by dragging them.
  • Demand a written note on the steps where "done" is not enough.
  • Duplicate one, when the next checklist is almost the same.
  • Archive and restore, so the active list stays short without losing anything.

Checklists live on their own and get attached to jobs. Changing a checklist here does not change the copy already sitting on a job that has been worked.

The Checklists list

To open it, go to Menu > Checklists.

Two tabs split the list: Active and Archived. Search sits in the card header and searches as you type.

ColumnWhat it shows
Date createdWhen it was built. Sortable.
TitleThe name, with its description underneath. Sortable.
OptionsThe row actions, which change with the tab you are on.

Clicking a row opens the checklist and its steps.

Creating a checklist

Click Add Checklist. The panel asks for a Checklist Title, which is required, and a Description, which is not.

Then the steps. Type a step and confirm it with the check on the right of the field or with Enter, and it drops into the list below. Add Item opens the field again for the next one.

A checklist cannot be saved empty: until it has at least one step, the panel says so and the save button stays disabled.

While you are still in the panel, each step can be edited or removed with the icons on its row.

Description Required

Each step carries a Description Required checkbox. Leave it off and the step is a tick: done or not done. Turn it on and the crew has to write what happened before the step counts.

Use it for the steps where the answer matters later: a meter reading, a serial number, the reason a part was replaced. It is also protection, because a step marked as requiring a description cannot be removed from a job once the checklist is attached.

The checklist

Open a checklist and you get its name, a pencil to rename it or change its description, a badge saying Active or Archived, and Add Item.

The steps sit in a table:

ColumnWhat it does
HandleDrag it to move the step. The new order saves by itself.
TitleThe step as the crew will read it.
Description RequiredThe dot turns the requirement on and off in place, with no panel to open.
EditChanges the wording of the step.
DeleteRemoves the step.

Two steps cannot be deleted, and the app says why when you hover the bin:

  • The last one standing. A checklist with no steps is not a checklist, so the final step stays.
  • One that requires a description. Turn the requirement off first, then delete it.

Order is worth a minute of your time. The crew reads the list top to bottom on a phone, so it should read like the work: what you do first, first.

Duplicating

The Duplicate action on a row copies the checklist with all its steps, and asks you to confirm first. It is the quick way to make the variant of a checklist you already have rather than typing it again.

Archiving, restoring and deleting

On the Active tab the row actions are Edit, Duplicate and Archive. Archiving asks you to confirm, then moves the checklist to the other tab. Nothing is lost, and jobs already carrying it are untouched.

On the Archived tab the actions are Edit, Restore and Delete. Restore brings it back to Active. Delete is permanent, and it is the only one of the three you cannot undo.

A checklist that is assigned to a job cannot be deleted at all. The bin is disabled and says so: the job would lose the steps it was worked with. Archive it instead.

Putting a checklist on a job

Checklists are attached to a job, not the other way round. You pick one in the first step of Add Job, or later through Edit job details on the job's Overview card.

From then on it lives in the job's Checklist tab, which is where the crew works it, usually from the mobile app on site. Steps can also be added there for that job alone, which does not touch the checklist you built here.

A job that carries a checklist will not complete in one click: the app puts the checklist in front of you and asks you to review it first. That is the whole point of having one.

See Jobs Management for working the checklist on site and completing the job.
