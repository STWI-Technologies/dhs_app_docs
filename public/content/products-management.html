Products Management

Overview

A product is something physical you stock, sell, or install — a filter, a thermostat, a length of pipe. Each one carries what it costs you, what you charge for it, and, if you want, how many you have. Add it to an estimate or an invoice and the price and the stock follow.

From this section you can:

  • Add products with cost, markup and price, a photo, and where they come from.
  • Track stock — what's on hand, what's already promised, when to reorder.
  • Edit one product, or bulk edit quantities across many.
  • Archive products you no longer carry, and restore them later.
  • Delete an archived product for good, when nothing references it.
  • Search and filter by tag, group, vendor, manufacturer and more.
  • Import and export your catalogue as CSV.

Important: archiving and deleting are different. Archiving takes a product out of the pickers while keeping every record that used it. Deleting is permanent, and only possible once nothing references the product.

The Products list

To open it, go to Menu > Inventory > Products.

Active and Archived

Two tabs: Active, what you currently carry, and Archived, what you've retired. Archived products don't appear when you're building an estimate or an invoice, but they stay on everything they were already part of.

Row actions

Each row has an eye that opens View product details — everything about the product, including its stock, read-only — and a three-dot menu with edit, archive or delete, and the rest.

Delete is greyed out while anything references the product: "This item can't be deleted because it's linked to existing records." Archive it instead.

Search and filters

The search box in the card header matches names and SKUs, and Filter opens a panel to narrow by tag, group, and the product's other attributes. Reset Filter clears them. Filters, search, and the tab you're on all work together.

Adding a product

Click Add Product. A panel opens from the right, titled Add New Product, with everything on one screen — the essentials first, then the details.

How cost, markup and price fit together

These three are linked, so you only ever fill in two:

  • Enter a cost and a markup, and the item price is worked out — cost plus that percentage.
  • Enter a cost and an item price, and the markup is worked out for you.

Which way you work depends on how you price. If you apply a standard margin, type the markup and let the price follow. If the price is fixed — by a manufacturer, or because that's what the market bears — type the price and see what margin it actually leaves you.

Stock

For anything you count, DHS tracks three numbers rather than one:

NumberWhat it means
Stock AvailableWhat you can still promise.
Stock Reserved for EstimatesAlready quoted, not yet committed.
Stock Reserved for JobsCommitted to work in progress.

The distinction is the point: ten filters on the shelf with eight already promised to jobs is not ten filters you can sell. Products show up as In Stock, Low stock, or Out of stock depending on where they sit against your re-order quantity.

Products you don't count

Not everything is worth counting — consumables, bulk fittings, anything you simply never run out of. Turn on Disable inventory tracking for those. They keep their price and work normally on estimates and invoices; they just don't carry quantities and won't warn you about stock.

Editing a product

Open the three-dot menu on the row and choose Edit product. Everything is editable except the SKU. Save with Apply Changes.

The edit panel also carries Archive (or Delete, for a product already archived), with a confirmation right there.

Updating quantities for several products at once

After a stock count or a delivery, editing products one at a time is a waste of an afternoon. Select the rows you want, and bulk edit lets you set Quantity On Hand and Re-Order Quantity for all of them together.

The panel tells you what it's about to do — how many products are selected, and whether any of them can't be updated. Products with inventory tracking switched off have no quantities to set, so they're left out; if none of your selection is tracked, you're told that instead of the change silently doing nothing. Turn tracking on in each product's edit panel first.

Archiving, restoring, deleting

To archive: three-dot menu > Archive > confirm. The product moves to the Archived tab and stops appearing in pickers. Everything that already used it is untouched. You can archive several at once by selecting their rows.

To restore: Archived tab > three-dot menu > Restore.

To delete for good: Archived tab > three-dot menu > Delete > confirm. Only available when the In Use column is empty.

Importing and exporting

Export CSV downloads your catalogue as a spreadsheet — for a price review, a stock count, or sending a vendor your list.

Import CSV brings a catalogue in, which is how you avoid typing hundreds of parts by hand. Upload the file, match your columns to the DHS fields, check the preview, and import. Rows that fail validation are reported so you can fix the file and try again.

Note: both buttons can be turned off for your account, in which case neither appears.

Products added in a hurry

Mid-estimate and the part isn't in your catalogue yet? Quick Add — from the top bar, or from inside the product picker — asks for a SKU, a name, and a price, creates it, and drops you back where you were. Its row carries a marker until someone fills in the vendor, the unit, and the quantities.

Where products are used

  • Estimates — quoted parts. Quantities you quote are held as reserved, so a quote can't promise stock twice.
  • Jobs — parts committed to work in progress.
  • Invoices — what the client is billed for, with tax on the products marked taxable.
  • Groups — a set of products and services you add together, instead of picking them one by one.

Changing a product's price changes what future records pick up; records already written keep the price they were created with.
