Estimates Management

Overview

An estimate is what you send a client before the work starts: what you'll do, what it costs, and how long the price holds. When they say yes, you turn it into a job in a couple of clicks, with no retyping.

From this section you can:

  • Build an estimate from your services and products, with a crew, a property, and a checklist.
  • Send it to the client by email, by text, or both.
  • Track where it stands: waiting for review, approved, changes requested, rejected.
  • Renew one that expired instead of starting again.
  • Convert it to a job once it's approved.
  • Duplicate one as the starting point for the next.
  • Download a PDF, or archive what you no longer need.
  • Set up a recurring estimate for work that repeats.

Search and filters

The search box in the card header finds estimates by name and client. Filter opens a panel to narrow by status and the estimate's other attributes, so "everything still waiting on a client" is one click away.

Creating an estimate

Click Add Estimate. The panel has two steps.

Step 1: the details

Who it's for and what it covers: the client and which of their properties, a name for the estimate, the crew who'd do the work, an expiration date, and a checklist if the job has one.

If the client isn't in DHS yet, Quick Add is right there in the picker, so you don't have to abandon the estimate to create them first.

Step 2: the line items

Now what you're charging for: the services and products that make up the work. Each comes in with its price already set from your catalogue, and you adjust the quantity.

If you've set up groups, add one and its whole set of items comes in at once instead of picking them one at a time. From there they're ordinary lines: change a quantity, drop what isn't needed.

Save, and the estimate starts as a Draft.

The estimate

Click an estimate to open it.

The header carries its name and status, and the buttons that matter for where it stands right now. A draft offers Submit estimate, an approved one offers Convert to job, an expired one offers Renew estimate. Behind More Actions: Duplicate, Download Pdf, and Delete.

The Overview tab is the estimate itself:

  • The details: title, crew, checklist, and the property, with Show Map to see where it is.
  • Services and Products: the lines, with quantities and prices. Add, change, or remove them here.
  • The bill: the subtotal, tax on whatever is marked taxable, and the total.
  • Notes: internal, for your team.
  • Chat: the conversation with the client about this estimate.
  • Attachments: photos, plans, anything that supports the quote.

The client sidebar keeps their phone and email to hand, so you can chase a quote without going to look them up.

When an estimate has a recurring schedule, a Recurring Schedule tab appears alongside Overview.

Sending an estimate to a client

Open the estimate and click Submit estimate.

Choose how it goes out: Email, SMS, or both. Email gets a subject and a message; SMS gets a short one. Both come with sensible default wording, so you can send without writing anything.

You can personalise the message with placeholders that get filled in for each client, so one piece of wording works for everyone. Message preview shows exactly what they'll receive, per channel, before it goes.

Once sent, the estimate moves to Ready for Review and the client can approve it, reject it, or ask for changes from their portal.

When a client asks for changes

The estimate comes back as Changes Requested. Edit it, adjusting lines, quantities or the price, and submit it again. It returns to Ready for Review.

When an estimate expires

Prices don't hold forever, which is why an estimate has an expiration date. Once it passes, the estimate becomes Expired and the page tells you so, along with the date it lapsed.

You don't have to start again: edit the estimate first, which is your chance to update prices that have moved since, and then renew it, and it goes back for review with a fresh validity period.

Turning an approved estimate into a job

This is the point of the whole thing. Once an estimate is Approved, Convert to job carries everything across: client, property, crew, services, products and checklist. Nothing is retyped and nothing is forgotten.

You're asked for the start date and then the end date, with dates the crew isn't available marked so you don't book over existing work. You can also flag the job as a priority.

When it's done, the estimate keeps a View Job link, so you can always get from the quote to the work that came out of it.

Duplicating

Quoting similar work over and over? Duplicate copies an estimate into a new draft, same lines and same prices, and you change the client and whatever else differs. Available from the row in the list and from inside the estimate.

Sharing a PDF

Download Pdf gives you the estimate as a document, for clients who want something to print, sign, or forward to whoever approves spending.

Archiving and deleting

Archiving clears an estimate off your active list while keeping it, which is what you want for quotes that went nowhere. They're on the Archived tab if you need them.

Deleting removes an estimate for good. Both are available from the list and from inside the estimate.
