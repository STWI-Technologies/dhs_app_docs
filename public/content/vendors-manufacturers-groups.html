Vendors, Manufacturers & Groups

Overview

Three short lists sit alongside your catalogue and make it easier to work with:

  • Vendors: who you buy from. Attach one to a product and reordering stops being a search through old invoices.
  • Manufacturers: who makes it. This is what answers a warranty call two years after the install.
  • Groups: sets of items that always go out together, so you add the whole set in one step instead of picking parts one by one.

All three live under Menu > Inventory, and all three work the same way: a list, a panel to add or edit, a read-only view, and a delete. None of them can be archived. Unlike products and services, these are either there or they aren't.

Vendors

Go to Menu > Inventory > Vendors.

The list shows each vendor's Name, Company, Phone, Email and Address. Each row has buttons to view the vendor's details, edit them, or delete the vendor.

Connecting a vendor to a product

A vendor on its own does nothing much. The value comes from the link: open a product, and set its Vendor and Vendor SKU, their code for the part, which is rarely yours. Combined with Average Delivery Time on the product, you know who to call, what to ask for, and how early to ask.

Vendor or manufacturer?

They're not the same thing and a product can have both. The manufacturer made the part; the vendor sold it to you. You might buy the same brand from three different suppliers, or one supplier might carry a dozen brands. On the product you record the manufacturer's part number and the vendor's SKU side by side: one to identify the part, the other to reorder it.

Groups

Go to Menu > Inventory > Groups.

A group is a bundle: the items that always go out together for a particular kind of work. A furnace replacement that's always the same unit, the same filter, the same thermostat and four hours of labour. Instead of adding each line by hand every time, you add the group.

The list shows the group's Group Code, Group Name, Description and SKU, and each row lets you view its details, edit it, or delete it.

Creating a group

Click Add Group:

FieldNotes
Group CodeA short code unique to this group. You'll be told if it's already taken.
Group NameWhat the bundle is for, in words someone else would recognise: "Furnace Replacement, Standard".
DescriptionWhat's in it and when to use it.
SKUThe items that make up the group.

Using a group

When you're building an estimate or an invoice, add the group and its items come in as individual lines, each with its own price, quantity, and tax setting. From that point they behave like any other line: change a quantity, drop one that isn't needed, leave the rest.

That's the important part: a group is a shortcut for adding items, not a link that stays. Editing the group later doesn't rewrite estimates you've already sent, and removing a line from an estimate doesn't touch the group. Groups save you the typing; the record is yours to adjust.

Products and services can also be filtered by group on their own lists, which is a quick way to see everything that belongs to one kind of job.

Deleting any of the three

Use the delete button on the row and confirm. There's no archive here and no undo, so before deleting a vendor or a manufacturer, remember that products point at them, so check what's linked first. Deleting a group removes the shortcut; the estimates and invoices built from it keep their lines.
